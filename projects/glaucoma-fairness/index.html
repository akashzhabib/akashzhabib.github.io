Addressing Bias in VLMs for Glaucoma Detection
Without Protected Attribute Supervision

Ahsan Habib Akash1 Greg Murray1 Annahita Amireskandari1 Joel Palko1 Carol Laxson1 Binod Bhattarai2,3,4 Prashnna K. Gyawali1

1West Virginia University2University of Aberdeen, UK3Fogsphere (Redev.AI Ltd), UK4University College London, UK

DEMI Workshop · MICCAI 2025
Paper arXiv Code — coming soon
TL;DRVision–language models for glaucoma screening can be unfair across demographic groups even when race or sex is never used in training. We mitigate this without any protected-attribute labels: cluster image embeddings to infer proxy subgroups, then up-weight the clusters the model handles worst during contrastive training.

Motivation

Glaucoma is a leading cause of irreversible blindness and disproportionately affects underserved populations. Automated screening from retinal fundus images with CLIP-style VLMs is promising, but these models can encode demographic bias. Most debiasing methods need protected-attribute labels, which are often missing, incomplete, or restricted in clinical data.

Method

We build on a reweighting contrastive-learning framework with three components:

Step 1
Proxy subgroups

Cluster image embeddings to infer latent subgroups, replacing protected-attribute labels.

Step 2
Gradient-balanced losses

Use gradient similarity to balance a CLIP-style image–text loss with a SimCLR-style image–image loss.

Step 3
Top-k reweighting

A top-k weighted objective puts more weight on the worst-performing clusters.

Results

We evaluate on the glaucoma subset of Harvard FairVLMed using Equalized Odds Distance (EOD), Equalized Subgroup AUC (ES-AUC), and Groupwise AUC.
